# Sāttvikābhinaya in Relation to Āṅgika and Vācika Abhinaya: A Critical Textual and Performative Analysis of Kuchipudi with Reference to Textual Traditions

*Extended Abstract — Doctoral Research Proposal*

## 1. Introduction and Background

> *Yato hastas tato dṛṣṭiḥ, yato dṛṣṭis tato manaḥ,*
> *Yato manas tato bhāvo, yato bhāvas tato rasaḥ.*
> — Nandikeśvara, *Abhinaya Darpaṇam*

This verse articulates a continuum that begins in the body (*hasta*), moves through the gaze (*dṛṣṭi*) into the mind (*manas*), crystallises as an emotional state (*bhāva*) and culminates in aesthetic relish (*rasa*). The present study locates itself precisely along this continuum, investigating **Sāttvikābhinaya** — the psycho-physical mode of expression — in its interdependence with **Āṅgikābhinaya** (bodily expression) and **Vācikābhinaya** (verbal/textual-musical expression) within the Kuchipudi tradition.

Dance, within the Indian aesthetic paradigm, is fundamentally a communicative act: every performance seeks to transmit meaning and emotional experience from the performer to the spectator. Bharata's *Nāṭyaśāstra* identifies four modes of *abhinaya* — *āṅgika*, *vācika*, *āhārya* and *sāttvika* — as the instruments of this communication. While *āṅgika* furnishes the gestural and kinetic vocabulary (*aṅga*, *pratyaṅga*, *upāṅga*, *hasta*) and *vācika* supplies the lyrical, musical and semantic content, it is *sāttvika* that animates these with inner conviction. The working premise of this research is that technically accurate *āṅgika* and *vācika*, in the absence of an authentic *sāttvika* component, remain incapable of effecting **rasa-niṣpatti** (the realisation of rasa) in the *sahṛdaya* (the sensitive spectator). Conversely, *sāttvika* is not an isolated faculty; it is articulated *through* and *made legible by* the *āṅgika* and *vācika* modes.

## 2. Conceptual Framework: The Notion of Sāttvikābhinaya

The term *sāttvikābhinaya* derives from **sattva** — in the *Nāṭyaśāstra*, a quality born of a concentrated, attentive mind (*samāhita manas*). Sāttvikābhinaya thus denotes the expression of emotion that arises within the performer and is outwardly manifested through involuntary or semi-voluntary psycho-physical states — the eight **sāttvika bhāvas**: *stambha* (stupefaction), *sveda* (perspiration), *romāñca* (horripilation), *svarabheda* (change of voice), *vepathu* (trembling), *vaivarṇya* (change of colour), *aśru* (tears) and *pralaya* (loss of consciousness).

Theoretically, this study engages with:

- **Bharata's Rasa-sūtra** — *vibhāvānubhāva-vyabhicāri-saṃyogād rasa-niṣpattiḥ* — which posits rasa as the outcome of the conjunction of determinants (*vibhāva*), consequents (*anubhāva*) and transitory states (*vyabhicāri bhāva*).
- **Abhinavagupta's** theory of **sādhāraṇīkaraṇa** (generalisation/universalisation) in the *Abhinavabhāratī*, through which the poet (*kavi*), performer (*naṭa*) and spectator (*sahṛdaya*) converge into a shared aesthetic consciousness.
- The idea of the performer's **tanmayībhāva** (identification) — the capacity to inhabit a character other than oneself while retaining aesthetic distance, thereby enabling the spectator to experience the same emotional state.

## 3. Scope of the Study

The research is delimited to the following thematic areas:

- Sāttvikābhinaya as the central analytical category
- Āṅgika and Vācika abhinaya as its correlative modes
- Kuchipudi **Nṛtya Nāṭikas** (dance-dramas), with *Bhāmākalāpam* as the primary case study
- **Padam** and **Jāvaḷi** as solo *abhinaya*-centric repertoire
- Classical treatises (*śāstra*) as the theoretical foundation

## 4. Research Gap and Problem Statement

Although every classical dancer performs in accordance with music and lyric, only some succeed in internalising *bhāva* and evoking *rasa*. A significant number of performers render facial expressions mechanically, without conscious awareness of which *bhāva* is being portrayed or which *rasa* is intended. Consequently, the performance may lack the transformative quality the tradition calls for. Younger practitioners in particular often express uncertainty regarding the appropriate expression for a given dramatic situation (*sandarbha*).

Existing scholarship tends either to treat the *śāstric* theory of rasa in abstraction from practice, or to document repertoire without systematically connecting it to that theory. There is a perceptible **gap in integrated, practice-oriented documentation** that demonstrates, step by step, how a Kuchipudi performer moves from the lyric (*sāhitya*) to its meaning, from meaning to *vibhāva–anubhāva–vyabhicāri*, and from these to the generation of rasa in the *rasika*.

> *"Vibhāvānubhāva-vyabhicāri-saṃyogād rasa-niṣpattiḥ"* — *Nāṭyaśāstra*, Ch. VI

This study seeks to address that gap by offering both a **theoretical framework** and a **performative model** for Sāttvikābhinaya in Kuchipudi.

## 5. Research Questions

1. How do the classical treatises define *sattva* and *sāttvikābhinaya*, and how do they position it relative to *āṅgika* and *vācika*?
2. In what ways do *āṅgika* and *vācika* abhinaya serve as the vehicles through which *sāttvika* becomes perceptible to the spectator?
3. How is the *bhāva → rasa* process realised in Kuchipudi *nṛtya nāṭikas* (specifically *Bhāmākalāpam*) and in *padam*/*jāvaḷi* repertoire?
4. How do contemporary Kuchipudi gurus and performers understand, train and experience *sāttvika* on stage?

## 6. Objectives

- To critically examine the textual sources on *sattva*, *abhinaya*, *bhāva* and *rasa*.
- To analyse the interrelationship between *sāttvika*, *āṅgika* and *vācika* abhinaya.
- To document how characters (*pātras*) and their *bhāvas* are developed in a Kuchipudi *nṛtya nāṭika*.
- To develop a lyric-to-rasa analytical model for *padam* and *jāvaḷi*.
- To create a theoretically grounded, practically applicable resource for young learners.

## 7. Research Methodology

The study adopts a **qualitative, interdisciplinary research design**, combining **textual-hermeneutic analysis** of Sanskrit and Telugu treatises with **performance analysis**, **ethnographic fieldwork** and **practice-as-research**. Findings from each strand will be **triangulated** to test textual theory against lived performance practice.

### 7.1 Methodological Framework (Tree Structure)

```
RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
│
├── PHASE I : Textual and Theoretical Inquiry (Śāstra)
│   ├── 1. Conceptual study of sattva, abhinaya and the four abhinaya modes
│   │      (āṅgika, vācika, āhārya, sāttvika)
│   ├── 2. Analysis of allied concepts
│   │      ├── Bhāva categories: sthāyi, vyabhicāri, sāttvika (49 bhāvas)
│   │      ├── Vibhāva (ālambana & uddīpana) and Anubhāva
│   │      ├── Rasa theory and sādhāraṇīkaraṇa
│   │      └── Nāyaka–Nāyikā bheda (incl. aṣṭa-nāyikā avasthās)
│   ├── 3. Comparative textual analysis across treatises
│   │      ├── Nāṭyaśāstra (Bharata)  ──► foundational definitions
│   │      ├── Abhinavabhāratī (Abhinavagupta) ──► rasa as aesthetic experience
│   │      ├── Abhinaya Darpaṇam (Nandikeśvara) ──► āṅgika vocabulary
│   │      ├── Bhāvaprakāśana (Śāradātanaya) ──► bhāva classification
│   │      ├── Śṛṅgāraprakāśa (Bhoja) ──► śṛṅgāra-centred rasa theory
│   │      └── Sāttvikābhinayam (P.S.R. Appa Rao) ──► Telugu/Kuchipudi perspective
│   └── 4. Identification of convergences, divergences and gaps between texts
│
├── PHASE II : Correlative Analysis of Abhinaya Modes
│   ├── 5. Āṅgika mapping
│   │      ├── Hasta (asaṃyuta / saṃyuta), dṛṣṭi, śiro-bheda, mukharāga
│   │      └── Correlating each gesture to the meaning it delivers
│   ├── 6. Vācika mapping
│   │      ├── Word-by-word (pada-artha) and contextual (bhāvārtha) meaning
│   │      ├── Role of rāga, tāla, kāku (intonation) and repetition (sañcārī)
│   │      └── Locating the lyrical cue points that trigger sāttvika response
│   └── 7. Sāttvika layering
│          └── Identifying where each of the eight sāttvika bhāvas can be
│              suggested through āṅgika and vācika means
│
├── PHASE III : Repertoire-based Case Studies (Performance Analysis)
│   ├── 8. Data collection
│   │      └── Sāhitya (lyrics), narrative, music, notation and audio-visual
│   │          recordings of selected nṛtya nāṭikas, padams and jāvaḷis
│   ├── 9. Case Study A — Kuchipudi Nṛtya Nāṭika: Bhāmākalāpam
│   │      ├── Character analysis of Satyabhāmā, Mādhavī and Kṛṣṇa
│   │      ├── Tracing bhāva transitions across scenes (Satyabhāmā's
│   │      │   praveśa daruvu, viraha, the letter episode, reconciliation)
│   │      ├── Interplay of vācika (dialogue/daruvu) and āṅgika
│   │      └── Identification of dominant (aṅgi) and subsidiary (aṅga) rasas
│   ├── 10. Case Study B — Padam / Jāvaḷi
│   │      ├── Word-by-word meaning of the lyric from pallavi to caraṇam
│   │      ├── Identification of nāyaka / nāyikā type and avasthā
│   │      ├── Mapping vibhāva → anubhāva → vyabhicāri → sthāyi → rasa
│   │      ├── Determining the principal rasa that "floats in the upper layer"
│   │      └── Comparative note: padam (restrained, introspective)
│   │          vs. jāvaḷi (lighter, more overtly sensuous)
│   └── 11. Video-based micro-analysis
│          └── Frame-by-frame study of recorded performances to observe how
│              sāttvika states are rendered through eyes, face and breath
│
├── PHASE IV : Ethnographic Fieldwork
│   ├── 12. Semi-structured interviews with Kuchipudi gurus, choreographers
│   │       and senior performers
│   │       ├── Their interpretation of śāstric theory
│   │       ├── Pedagogical methods for training sāttvika
│   │       └── Personal anubhava (lived experience) of rasa on stage
│   ├── 13. Participant observation of classes, rehearsals and lecture-demonstrations
│   └── 14. Spectator (sahṛdaya) response study
│          └── Short questionnaires / discussions with informed audiences on the
│              bhāva and rasa they perceived in selected performances
│
├── PHASE V : Practice-as-Research
│   ├── 15. Researcher's own performance of selected items, with reflective
│   │       journaling on the internal process (from lyric to rasa)
│   └── 16. Comparative presentation: the same item rendered with
│           (a) āṅgika–vācika alone, and (b) integrated sāttvika,
│           to demonstrate the qualitative difference
│
└── PHASE VI : Synthesis and Model Building
    ├── 17. Triangulation of textual, performative and ethnographic findings
    ├── 18. Formulation of a "Lyric-to-Rasa" analytical model for Kuchipudi
    └── 19. Preparation of a practitioner-oriented guide for young learners
```

### 7.2 The Proposed Analytical Model (Lyric-to-Rasa)

```
Sāhitya (Lyric)
   └─► Pada-artha (literal meaning)
         └─► Bhāvārtha (contextual / emotional meaning)
               └─► Vibhāva (ālambana: the character / uddīpana: the setting)
                     └─► Anubhāva (āṅgika + vācika expression)
                           └─► Sāttvika bhāva (inner state made visible)
                                 └─► Vyabhicāri bhāvas (transient states)
                                       └─► Sthāyi bhāva (dominant emotion)
                                             └─► RASA in the Sahṛdaya
```

### 7.4 Ethical Considerations

Informed consent will be obtained from all interviewees; recordings will be used only for research purposes, and the contributions of gurus and traditional lineages (*paramparā*) will be duly acknowledged.

## 8. Expected Outcomes and Significance

- A **critical synthesis** of śāstric perspectives on Sāttvikābhinaya and its relationship to Āṅgika and Vācika.
- A **documented, scene-wise bhāva–rasa analysis** of *Bhāmākalāpam* and selected *padams/jāvaḷis*.
- A **replicable Lyric-to-Rasa model** that young Kuchipudi practitioners can apply to any abhinaya composition.
- A contribution to **dance pedagogy** by bridging the gap between *śāstra* (theory) and *prayoga* (practice), helping learners understand not only *what* expression to give, but *why* and *how* it generates rasa.
